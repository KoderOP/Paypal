# FINORA — Personal Financial Digital Twin

**Don't just track your money. Simulate your financial future.**

FINORA builds a working model of a person's finances from their transaction history (income cycles, recurring commitments and
day-to-day spending) and uses it to project their balance day by day. Before a decision is made, it shows the effect of that
decision on future cash flow: a large purchase, a delayed salary, a cancelled subscription or an unexpected bill.

Many personal-finance tools focus on what has already happened. FINORA addresses the moment before money moves. It answers
questions such as *"Can I afford this, and when?"* or *"What happens if my income is two weeks late?"* with a computed,
day-by-day projection of the outcome.

Two parts work together:

- A **deterministic financial engine** computes every figure: forecasts, scenario outcomes, payment comparisons and stress
  tests. Monetary values use `Decimal` arithmetic, and identical inputs always produce identical results.
- An **AI assistant** interprets questions in natural language, selects the appropriate engine calculations and explains the
  results. A guardrail checks every amount, percentage and date in its answers against the engine's output.

FINORA connects these insights to **PayPal**: it reads transaction history through PayPal's Transaction Search API, and it can
prepare a PayPal Orders v2 checkout for a chosen payment option. That checkout is completed only after the user acknowledges
the projected impact, approves the payment on PayPal and explicitly confirms the capture.

## Product Overview

### 1. Financial Digital Twin
- **Transaction analysis:** imports PayPal transaction history, PayPal Activity reports, bank statements and generic
  transaction files, normalised into a single ledger with the source of every record retained.
- **Financial profile:** detects recurring income and recurring commitments (rent, bills, subscriptions, investments), and
  estimates variable spending per category from recent history.
- **Health indicators:** savings rate, emergency runway, fixed-cost ratio, subscription load and lowest projected balance,
  each assessed against explicit thresholds.
- **Cash-flow view:** recorded balance history alongside a projected trajectory, monthly income and spending, and upcoming
  payments.

### 2. Predictive Financial Intelligence
- **90-day forecast:** a day-by-day balance projection from recurring income, commitments, planned obligations and estimated
  daily spending.
- **Upcoming obligations:** bills, instalments and planned purchases appear as dated events, with proactive alerts (a balance
  falling below the safety buffer, a large bill due before income, late income, subscription price increases).
- **Affordability:** the maximum amount that can be spent today, this month or within a window without falling below the
  safety buffer.
- **Optimal timing:** the earliest safe date for a purchase, evaluating each candidate date over the full horizon that
  follows it.

### 3. What-if Scenario Simulation
- Purchases, delayed or reduced income (optionally for a single client), cancelled or new subscriptions, unexpected expenses,
  higher savings, spending changes and exchange-rate movements.
- Changes can be combined into a single scenario. Each scenario is compared with the baseline over 30, 60 and 90 days, with
  the lowest projected balance and any period below the buffer identified.
- Up to four scenarios can be compared side by side, and scenarios can be saved for later comparison.

### 4. Financial Stress Testing
- **Shock library:** job loss for one or three months, a 30% pay cut, a medical emergency, living costs up 30%, income 30
  days late, and a currency drop for foreign income.
- **Spending variability:** 400 seeded Monte Carlo paths based on the user's own month-to-month and day-to-day variation,
  shown as 10th–90th percentile balance bands.
- **Resilience measures:** emergency runway, probability of falling below the safety buffer or zero, and a resilience rating
  (strong, moderate or fragile) derived from explicit thresholds.

### 5. AI Financial Assistant
- **Natural-language queries:** questions about purchases, affordability, scenarios, resilience and foreign income, with
  follow-ups that build on the previous answer.
- **Tool orchestration:** the model selects from seven engine tools and supplies their arguments; it can combine several
  calculations in one answer.
- **Grounded answers:** amounts, percentages and dates in each answer are validated against the engine's output. A draft
  containing an unsupported figure is replaced with the engine's own wording, and the response indicates this.
- **Resilience to provider failure:** retries, a fallback model and, if needed, a deterministic planner that answers common
  questions with the same tools. Model and token details are available under *Technical details*.

### 6. PayPal Integration
- **Transaction synchronisation:** read-only Transaction Search with idempotent imports keyed on PayPal transaction ID.
  Records in a foreign currency are converted at the configured rate or skipped.
- **Checkout workflow:** Orders v2 checkout with three user-controlled steps: acknowledgement of the projected impact in
  FINORA, buyer approval on PayPal, and explicit capture confirmation in FINORA. The captured payment updates the financial
  twin.
- **Pay Later modelling:** Pay in 4 and Pay Monthly options are modelled from illustrative terms to compare their cash-flow
  effect. PayPal determines actual Pay Later eligibility, APR and schedule on its own approval page.
- **Subscriptions:** subscriptions are detected from history, added manually or linked through the PayPal Subscriptions
  API, where they can be refreshed, cancelled, suspended or reactivated. Changes to a subscription require explicit
  confirmation.
- **Authentication:** optional Log in with PayPal (OpenID Connect). No feature requires sign-in.
- **Integration status:** the PayPal Hub reports a live credential test, the last synchronisation result, record counts by
  source, and each checkout with its PayPal order and capture IDs.
- **Demo mode:** without credentials, an in-process stand-in for the PayPal API runs the same client code. It is clearly
  labelled, never contacts PayPal and never moves money.

### 7. Cross-border Freelancer Finance
- **Multi-currency income:** foreign-currency payments retain their original amount, PayPal fee and applied conversion rate.
- **Fee and conversion analysis:** income by client, PayPal fees, the average rate applied, and conversion cost against a
  user-entered reference rate.
- **Sensitivity:** the effect of an exchange-rate movement and of a client paying late.

## Example: Evaluating a ₹75,000 Purchase

A user asks: *"Should I buy a ₹75,000 laptop?"* FINORA simulates each way of paying against the sample profile, with a
₹20,000 safety buffer. Figures below are from a run on 12 Nov 2026; the sample profile is generated relative to the current
date, so exact amounts vary slightly, while the outcome is consistent across dates.

| Option | Lowest projected balance | Effect on the safety buffer |
|---|---|---|
| Pay in full today | ₹13,260 on 23 Nov 2026 | Below the buffer for 7 days before the next salary |
| Wait until payday (24 Nov) | ₹33,252 | Stays above |
| Pay in 4 (₹18,750 today, then 3 × ₹18,750) | ₹52,002 | Stays above, no additional cost |
| Pay Monthly, 12 months | ₹1,01,305 | Stays above, ₹8,367 additional cost at 19.99% APR |

Paying in full leaves the balance below the buffer for a week, because bills and day-to-day spending fall due before the
next salary arrives. Waiting until payday is safe but delays the purchase. Pay in 4 keeps the balance above the buffer at no additional
cost, so FINORA recommends it. The Pay in 4 and Pay Monthly figures use illustrative terms.

If the user proceeds, FINORA prepares a checkout proposal. Nothing is created or charged until the user acknowledges the
impact. FINORA then creates the order through the PayPal Orders API, the user approves it on PayPal, and the payment is
captured only after explicit confirmation. The resulting payment or instalment schedule is then reflected in the forecast.

The same engine evaluates any decision in the same way: a different purchase, a salary delay, a new subscription or a
combination of changes.

## How FINORA Works

1. **Financial data ingestion.** Transactions arrive from PayPal Transaction Search, PayPal Activity reports, bank statements,
   generic transaction files or the sample profile.
2. **Normalisation and categorisation.** Every source is converted into one transaction format and categorised by rules.
   Each record keeps its source, and duplicates are skipped on transaction ID.
3. **Profile and digital twin construction.** Recurring income and commitments are detected, variable spending is estimated
   per category, and balances are derived from opening balances plus transactions.
4. **Forecast generation.** Recurring items, obligations and estimated daily spending become dated cash events, projected day
   by day over a 90-day horizon with explicit assumptions.
5. **Scenario simulation.** A decision is expressed as changes to those events and projected with the same engine, then
   compared with the baseline.
6. **AI-assisted interpretation.** The assistant selects which calculations to run and explains the results. The engine
   computes every figure, and the guardrail verifies the answer.
7. **Payment proposal and authorised execution.** For a chosen option, FINORA prepares a PayPal checkout proposal. The order
   is created only after acknowledgement and captured only after PayPal approval and explicit confirmation.

The AI orchestrates; it does not calculate. Steps 3 to 5 are deterministic and run without any language model.

## Architecture

FINORA consists of a React single-page application and a FastAPI backend organised in four layers: API routers, a services
layer for data access, a pure financial engine, and the AI assistant. A PayPal integration package handles all PayPal
communication, and all external calls (Groq and PayPal) originate from the backend.

```
React frontend ──/api──▶ FastAPI routers ──┬──▶ services ──┬──▶ financial engine
                                           │               └──▶ SQLAlchemy ──▶ SQLite / PostgreSQL
                                           ├──▶ AI assistant ──▶ Groq API   (its tools call the engine)
                                           └──▶ PayPal integration ──▶ PayPal REST API
```

The detailed design, including diagrams of the data pipeline, forecasting, scenario simulation, AI assistant and payment
lifecycle, is documented in [Detailed Architecture](architecture.md).

## Security and Financial Integrity

| Safeguard | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Decimal monetary calculations | All money is `Decimal`; the `money()` helper rejects floats; database columns are `NUMERIC(14,2)` |
| Server-side validation | Every request and every AI tool argument is validated with Pydantic before any calculation |
| AI grounding | Amounts, percentages and dates in AI answers must match engine output; drafts that fail are replaced and flagged |
| Acknowledgement before order creation | `POST /api/checkout/orders` is rejected without `acknowledged: true`; options below the buffer require an additional confirmation in the interface |
| PayPal buyer approval | Capture is rejected unless the order has been approved on PayPal; approval is re-verified with PayPal on return |
| Explicit capture confirmation | Capture is rejected without `confirm: true` |
| Duplicate capture protection | A captured order returns its existing capture; PayPal calls carry a `PayPal-Request-Id` idempotency key |
| AI action boundary | The assistant can only prepare a checkout proposal; no AI tool creates an order or captures a payment |
| Secrets on the backend | API keys and PayPal credentials are read from server-side environment variables and never sent to the browser |
| Request protection | Per-client chat rate limit, security headers on every response, CORS restricted to configured origins, HMAC-signed session cookies |
| Data provenance | Sample, imported and PayPal records are labelled by source and cannot be combined into one ledger |

Never commit `.env` files or credentials. Use PayPal Sandbox credentials for development and demonstrations.

## Getting Started

### Prerequisites
- Python 3.12 or later
- Node.js 20.19+ or 22.12+ (required by Vite)
- Docker and Docker Compose (optional)

### Environment configuration

```bash
cp .env.example .env    # repository root; backend/.env is also read
```

No configuration is required to run FINORA locally. Without keys, the assistant uses the deterministic planner, PayPal runs
in demo mode and SQLite is used. Optional settings:

| Variable | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `GROQ_API_KEY` | Enables the AI assistant (`GROQ_MODEL`, `GROQ_FALLBACK_MODEL` to override models) |
| `PAYPAL_CLIENT_ID`, `PAYPAL_CLIENT_SECRET` | PayPal REST credentials; `PAYPAL_BASE_URL` defaults to the Sandbox |
| `PAYPAL_CHECKOUT_CURRENCY`, `PAYPAL_CHECKOUT_FX_RATE` | Checkout currency and the conversion rate from the profile currency |
| `DATABASE_URL` | Defaults to SQLite; use `postgresql+psycopg://...` for PostgreSQL |
| `FRONTEND_URL`, `CORS_ORIGINS` | Public application URL (used for PayPal return links) and allowed origins |
| `SESSION_SECRET` | Signing key for Log in with PayPal sessions |

The complete list, with defaults, is in [`.env.example`](.env.example).

### Backend

```bash
cd backend
python3 -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
uvicorn app.main:app --reload --port 8000
```

The API is available at `http://localhost:8000`, with interactive documentation at `http://localhost:8000/docs`.

### Frontend

```bash
cd frontend
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open `http://localhost:5173`. The Vite development server proxies `/api` to the backend (`VITE_API_PROXY_TARGET`, default
`http://localhost:8000`). Select **Launch the live demo** on the landing page to load the sample profile.

### Docker

```bash
docker compose up --build
```

This starts PostgreSQL 16, the backend on port 8000 and the frontend on port 5173. The backend reads an optional `.env` from
the repository root. The backend image contains only the application package, so committed evaluation results are not shown
on the Evaluation page when running in Docker.

### Database
- **SQLite** (default): `backend/finora.db` is created on first start.
- **PostgreSQL**: set `DATABASE_URL=postgresql+psycopg://user:password@host:5432/finora`.

Tables are created at startup, and columns added in later versions are applied to existing databases automatically.

### PayPal Sandbox
1. In the PayPal Developer Dashboard, create a Sandbox app and copy its client ID and secret into `.env`. Enable
   **Transaction search** in the app's features for synchronisation.
2. The tested Sandbox flow does not accept INR orders. Set `PAYPAL_CHECKOUT_CURRENCY=USD` and `PAYPAL_CHECKOUT_FX_RATE` (INR
   per USD, for example `84`, which charges ₹75,000 as USD 892.86). The rate is user-configured, not retrieved from PayPal.
3. Approve test payments with a US-registered Sandbox personal account. India-registered Sandbox buyer accounts were declined
   in testing.
4. Verify the configuration from `backend/` with the virtual environment active: `python -m evals.check_paypal` performs
   OAuth, creates a USD 1.00 order and, after approval, captures it with `--capture <order id>`.

To verify a Groq key and model, run `python -m evals.check_groq` from `backend/`. The Groq free tier allows 8,000 tokens per
minute, and a question uses roughly 4,000–4,800 tokens; when the limit is reached, the fallback model or the deterministic
planner answers.

### Running tests

From the repository root:

```bash
(cd backend && .venv/bin/python -m pytest -q)          # backend test suite
(cd frontend && npx oxlint src && npm run build)       # lint, type-check and production build
```

## Evaluation and Testing

FINORA includes four reproducible evaluations, run from `backend/` with `python -m evals.run_all` (add `--llm` to include the
live Groq benchmark).
**They use controlled data: synthetic histories and the fictional sample profile. They demonstrate the method and do not
measure accuracy for real customers.** Results are also available in the application's Evaluation page.

| Evaluation | Result | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| AI tool selection: live Groq, `openai/gpt-oss-120b`, 40 questions | **92.5%** correct tool, **90.0%** correct arguments | Held-out paraphrases: 78.6% tool, 71.4% arguments |
| AI tool selection: deterministic planner, same questions | 85.0% correct tool, 77.5% correct arguments | Held-out paraphrases: 57.1% tool, 35.7% arguments |
| Answer grounding | 100% of final answers grounded in engine figures (both modes) | Live run: 17.5% of drafts rewritten by the guardrail (7.5–20% across three runs); 15% answered by the planner after temporary provider errors |
| Guardrail red-team | **97.7%** of 262 deliberately corrupted drafts detected; 0% of 154 correct drafts rejected | 76.3% detected with a flat 1% tolerance |
| Forecast backtest (synthetic, noise-injected) | **95.0%** of purchase-safety decisions correct; 3.5% false-safe rate | Seasonal-naive baseline: 92.6% and 4.6% |

**Methodology.** The agent benchmark contains 40 single- and multi-turn questions, 14 of them paraphrases held out from the
planner's development. The forecast backtest uses 60 synthetic six-month histories (two personas), five walk-forward cut-offs
each (300 forecasts per model) and a 30-day horizon; on clean histories the engine reaches 96.8% against 93.2% for the
seasonal-naive baseline. The red-team rewrites correct answers in valid formats (lakh notation, rounding, alternative date
styles), which must pass, and corrupts single claims, which must be detected. Full tables and methodology are in
[docs/EVALUATION.md](docs/EVALUATION.md).

**Tests.** The backend suite contains 207 tests covering the engine, file parsers, PayPal flows (mocked HTTP and demo mode),
the Groq client's retry and fallback behaviour, the agent loop, the guardrail, data provenance and reconciliation, payment
safeguards, and the sample scenario across a full year of dates. Tests never read local `.env` files.
